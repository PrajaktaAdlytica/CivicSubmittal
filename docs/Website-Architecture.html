01 Project direction and scope

CivicSubmittal is an applicant-side construction permit coordination concept in the logistics and knowledge sector. Its first commercial hypothesis is supervised packet-readiness review for one municipality and one repeatable commercial tenant-improvement workflow. The buyer is a builder operations director or expediting-firm principal; the daily user is a permit coordinator. Licensed architects retain professional decisions; city officials retain permitting decisions.

This deliverable specifies the complete website and isolated synthetic demo. It is an information architecture and design plan, not a built website or production permitting product. The requested $100,000 sale ambition is treated as a craftsmanship and business ambition, not a verified valuation, SaaS price, budget or revenue claim.

The latest direct request governs: use CivicSubmittal, preserve every substantive brief requirement, and apply the uploaded master guide as the website quality and completeness standard. The guide itself says briefs take precedence; your direct instruction reverses that document-level default. Its full website architecture is retained, while factual stage labels and professional boundaries remain accurate. The brief’s earlier “spec only” and later prototype instructions are reconciled here as a specification for a future concept website and demo.

Permit Waypoint and permitwaypoint.com are legacy brief references, not the chosen brand or an approved domain. The historical RDAP 404, unpurchased-domain status and absent trademark clearance are preserved as legacy research notes only. No present availability or legal clearance is inferred. No video or source screenshot was supplied in this chat; the referenced screenshots/43-website-preview.png cannot be treated as inspected evidence.

04 Positioning and content hierarchy

Hero headline: Know what your next submission is missing.

Supporting copy: Explore a proposed applicant workspace for checking permit packets against cited local requirements, resolving deficiencies with your architect, and preparing for human submission. Starting with one municipality and one repeatable tenant-improvement workflow.

Tagline retained: An applicant-side packet readiness map for one jurisdiction and one repeatable permit type.

Vision: Project teams understand the authority, drawings and predecessor approvals governing the next submission before rework occurs. Mission: Translate cited local requirements into supervised checklists and reviewer-response tracking, without standing in for the authority having jurisdiction.

Primary CTA: Discuss a packet-review pilot. Secondary CTA: Explore the synthetic demo. On a validated jurisdiction page, the pilot CTA can say “Request a review for an upcoming packet.” Never use “Get approved,” “Instant permit,” or an implied municipal endorsement.

Use a discreet persistent “Research-stage concept” status on marketing pages. Every product preview carries “CONCEPT PREVIEW” and “DEMO — SYNTHETIC DATA.” Proposed module labels appear next to product names, and test prices carry their hypothesis qualifier beside the number.
